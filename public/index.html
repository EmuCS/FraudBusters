Four questions, answered before anyone pays

Is it real?

Three shots captured live on the claimant’s phone, from a side the server picks at random. Signed on the device and recorded on Solana the moment they are taken. Any later edit breaks the record.

Is it new?

Checked against every claim on the shared registry, at every insurer, even after compression. Plus the claimant’s claims across the whole market.

Does it add up?

AI compares the photos with the description: same object in every shot, a real scene and not a screen, damaged parts and severity, and whether the amount fits.

Settled?

Under the insurer’s fast-track limit with every check passed: paid in seconds. Otherwise an adjuster decides, signs with their wallet, and the payment settles in stablecoins.

Why a public record, not just an upload

Nobody can backdate it

A server's records can be changed by whoever runs it. On Solana the capture time is fixed when the photo is taken, so damage from before the policy started can’t be passed off as new.

Shared, without an owner

Duplicate claims only show up if every insurer checks the same list. Like a car history check: anyone can look up a fingerprint, nobody sees photos, names or claim details.

Proof for both sides

The claim details are sealed on the record. The claimant can prove what they sent and when; the insurer can prove every decision and every automatic payment followed its signed rule.
